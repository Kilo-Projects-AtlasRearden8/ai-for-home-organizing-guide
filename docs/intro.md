# Getting Started with AI for Home Organizing

You do not need to be technical to use AI at home. If you can open a website
and type a sentence, you can do everything in this manual.

## What AI is, plainly

Think of AI as a very well-read assistant who has read a lot of text and can
help you write, sort, plan, and brainstorm. It is not magic, and it is not
always right. You are the boss. You check its work before you act on it.

## Where to start

You have three easy options, in order from simplest to most private:

1. A free web chatbot — Open a browser, sign up for a free account at a major
   AI provider, and start typing. Fastest way to try it. Not private enough for
   sensitive stuff.
2. A local AI app — Download a free program that runs AI on your own computer
   (for example, LM Studio or Ollama). Nothing leaves your machine. Slightly
   more setup, much more private.
3. AI built into tools you already use — Your phone, email, and photo apps may
   already have AI features. Check your settings before installing anything new.

## How to talk to AI (prompts, plainly)

You do not need special commands. Just ask like you would ask a friend.

> "Help me make a simple weekly cleaning schedule for a family of four. Keep
> it short and doable."

Or:

> "I have a pile of papers on the kitchen counter. Give me three folders to
> sort them into, and what goes in each."

If the answer is not quite right, say so:

> "That's too long. Make it shorter and add a Sunday reset step."

## A simple starter routine

Pick one small task this week:

- Monday: Ask AI to help you sort one drawer or shelf.
- Wednesday: Ask AI to plan three dinners using what is already in your fridge.
- Friday: Ask AI to write a one-page list of where your important papers live
  (without writing down account numbers).

Small wins build momentum. That is the whole idea.
